# Concentrated Stock Playbook

**Advisory playbook 12, updated 2026-03. Trigger: any single equity above 10% of household investable assets.**

## Framing the conversation

Lead with math, not fear. A single stock at 30%+ of a portfolio roughly doubles portfolio volatility versus a diversified sleeve with similar expected return. Anchor on the client's goals ("your financial-independence target is fully fundable if we de-risk") rather than on a price prediction for the stock. Many concentrated holders are emotionally attached — often employees or long-term believers. Validate the conviction, then quantify the cost of being wrong.

## The five tools, in escalation order

1. **Staged sale program.** Pre-committed calendar sales (e.g., 10% of the position per quarter for 8 quarters). Simple, transparent, dollar-cost-averages the exit. Pair with loss harvesting elsewhere to offset gains. Works best inside a written plan the client signs.
2. **Exchange fund.** Contribute appreciated shares to a pooled vehicle in exchange for a diversified basket; defers capital gains. Requires 7-year lockup, qualified-purchaser status, and 20%+ illiquid sleeve in the fund. Minimum contribution on our platform: $500k. Best for very low basis stock where the client refuses outright sales.
3. **Equity collar.** Buy a protective put, finance it by selling a covered call (e.g., 90% floor / 110% cap, 12 months). Locks a value range without a sale; no upfront tax event. Watch for constructive-sale rules if the band is too tight. Suitable for bridging to a known liquidity date (option vesting, exchange-fund entry window).
4. **Charitable strategies.** Gift appreciated shares to a donor-advised fund or charitable remainder trust: removes the gain, produces a deduction, and funds existing philanthropic goals. Particularly efficient for clients with standing charitable intent.
5. **Qualified opportunity / direct-indexing transition.** Sell in tranches and redeploy into a direct-indexing sleeve that harvests losses to shelter the remaining gains over 3–5 years.

## Employer-stock specifics

For active employees and insiders: confirm trading-window and 10b5-1 plan requirements before any program; coordinate with the company's stock-admin team. Upcoming vests should be sold at vest by default ("sell-to-diversify") since vest-date sales carry no additional tax cost versus holding.

## Documentation requirements

Present at least two of the five tools in writing within 90 days of the concentration flag. Record the client's decision — including a decision to keep the position — in CRM with the suitability rationale. Re-present annually while the position stays above 10%.

## Worked example (anonymized pattern)

Client, mid-40s tech executive, employer-adjacent chip stock at ~35% of investable assets, basis under one-fifth of market price. Recommended package: sell-to-diversify on all future vests, a 2-year staged sale of one-third of the existing position, and an exchange-fund contribution for another third, leaving one-third as a conviction hold. Net effect: concentration below 15% within 24 months with capital gains spread across three tax years.
